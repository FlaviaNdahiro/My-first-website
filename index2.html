Choosing what gets styled

A selector is the part of a CSS rule before the curly braces. It answers one question: which elements does this rule apply to? Three of them do most of the work.

1. Descendant selector — .card p

A space between two selectors means anywhere inside. Every paragraph inside the card is matched, no matter how deeply it is nested.

2. Child selector — .card > p

A > means direct child only. A paragraph wrapped in another <div> would be skipped.

3. Adjacent sibling — h3 + p

A + matches only the single element that comes immediately after, at the same level. It is the selector behind “style the first paragraph after a heading”.

Quick reference

Selector Reads as Matches
.card p descendant Any p inside .card, at any depth
.card > p child Only p one level down
h3 + p adjacent sibling The one p right after an h3
h3 ~ p general sibling Every later p sharing the parent
a:hover pseudo-class A link while the pointer is on it
Keep demos scoped. A bare h3 + p rule would reach into every page of this site. Prefixing it with .selector-demo keeps the experiment inside its box.
